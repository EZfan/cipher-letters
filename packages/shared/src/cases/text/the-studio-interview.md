# A Conversation with Vivian Lassiter

_Recorded in a small studio on West 23rd Street, Manhattan, October 1998. The interviewer has chosen to remain anonymous._

---

**INTERVIEWER.** _Let's begin with the obvious question. Where have you been?_

**VIVIAN.** _Where has it been?_ It has been, I think, in a great many rooms, none of which I would have wanted to call home. It has been in a small rehearsal space on the lower east side. It has been in the back of a taxi on a Tuesday afternoon. It has been in the throat. Mostly, it has been in the throat.

**INTERVIEWER.** _For fifteen years?_

**VIVIAN.** _It does not like the way you said that. It does not like the number. The number is an instrument, you understand, and the instrument was not played. It was held. Held for a long time. I would not call that absence. I would call that_ waiting.

**INTERVIEWER.** _Some people would call that absence._

**VIVIAN.** _Some people are not the person who was holding it._ _(She looks toward the booth.)_ J., are we rolling?

**J.** _(From the booth, off-tape.)_ We're rolling, Vivian. We're always rolling.

**VIVIAN.** _Then we are always, in a sense, on the record. I am not sure I like that._

**INTERVIEWER.** _The album._

**VIVIAN.** _The album._

**INTERVIEWER.** _You recorded it alone?_

**VIVIAN.** _I recorded it in this room. The room is — the room was — a room I have known for a long time. It is a kind of home for the voice. It has been well kept. The microphone is the better one, the newer one, the one I requested from the label. It is the one I have been using for the album. It hears what I need it to hear._

**INTERVIEWER.** _You have written almost all the songs yourself._

**VIVIAN.** _I have written — I have done — a great deal of the writing. Some of the songs were — they were already — some of the songs were finished. I did not have to finish them. They were waiting, also._

**INTERVIEWER.** _For you to find them?_

**VIVIAN.** _For me to sing them. To let them out._ _(A pause.)_ _I think that is what a voice is for. It is for letting things out._

**INTERVIEWER.** _You have been asked, in other interviews, about your sister._

**VIVIAN.** _I have been asked._

**INTERVIEWER.** _You have not, in those interviews, given her name._

**VIVIAN.** _I have not given her name. I do not give her name in interviews. The name is not — the name is hers. I will not say it here. The microphone is the better one. The microphone will remember it. The microphone will hold it. I will not._

**INTERVIEWER.** _She died fifteen years ago. The fire at the rehearsal space on Houston Street._

**VIVIAN.** _(Long silence.)_ _The years, you said. The years._

**INTERVIEWER.** _The fire, I said._

**VIVIAN.** _Yes. The years. The fire is — the years are — I do not — *(She lights a cigarette, her first in many years, the journalist noted in the margin.)* — the years took her. The years. The fire is a word. I do not use it._

**INTERVIEWER.** _The album, then, is a tribute._

**VIVIAN.** _The album is — I am not sure the word "tribute" is the right one. A tribute is something you do from outside. The album is not — it is not outside. It is — the album is hers, in the sense that — the album is not mine. It is hers. I am only the — *(She looks toward the booth again.)* — the one through whom it — passes._

**INTERVIEWER.** _The microphone is the new one._

**VIVIAN.** _The microphone is the new one. The better one. I requested it from the label. They sent it. I have been using it for the album. It is the only microphone I will use._

**INTERVIEWER.** _J., in the booth, has worked with you for a long time._

**VIVIAN.** _J. has known the voice for a long time. J. was — J. is kind. J. remembers. J. is the only person in this city who knew — who knows — *(She stops.)* — J. is a friend._

**INTERVIEWER.** _Of hers._

**VIVIAN.** _Of — yes. J. was a friend of — J. was a friend. J. was a friend of hers. J. was a friend of mine. They are the same — they were — I am — *(She stubs out the cigarette.)* — I am two people, in a sense. The other one is not here. She is, however, in the room._

**INTERVIEWER.** _Vivan, this is —_

**VIVIAN.** _Do not say her name. I am asking you, very specifically, not to say her name in this room. The microphone will hold it. The microphone is the better one. I will not have her name held by a microphone._

**INTERVIEWER.** _All right. I won't._

**VIVIAN.** _Thank you. Thank you. That is — the kettle is — *(She gestures toward a hot-plate at the edge of the frame. There is, in fact, a kettle there. It is not new.)* — I am sorry. I have been — the years are — they are —_

---

_(At this point, the building's fire alarm went off. The recording stopped. The studio engineer, J., went into the booth. Vivian Lassiter was, he said later, sitting perfectly still in the chair, looking at the microphone as if she had forgotten how to stand up. When the fire department arrived, there was no fire. The alarm had been triggered by a small device inside the studio, taped to the inside of the door, which Vivian had, it later emerged, brought in her bag. She did not give an interview for the remainder of 1998. The album was released in February of the following year to wide critical acclaim. The microphone used for the interview was identified, by a careful listener, as the same model of microphone used on every Clara Lassiter recording between 1980 and 1983.)_
